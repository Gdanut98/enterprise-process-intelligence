# Case Study — Enterprise Proposal Cycle-Time Optimization

## Define
The objective was to reduce proposal-creation cycle time by 15% while improving process consistency and customer/seller experience. VOC and CTQ thinking translated complaints about slow proposals, handoffs, incomplete information, pricing and approvals into measurable process requirements.

## Measure
The baseline measurement plan included mean/median end-to-end cycle time, SLA compliance, DPMO, sigma level, process-step time, distribution shape, and defect/rework indicators. A proposal exceeding 35 days was treated as a defect.

## Analyze
Performance was segmented by geography, brand, seller, bid-support specialist and bid size. Histograms, boxplots, Pareto analysis and step-level timing were used to distinguish broad variation from concentrated bottlenecks. The process narrative suggested approval delay, routing, incomplete requests, skill mismatch and redundant handoffs as hypotheses to test.

## Improve
The improvement logic focused on risk-based approvals, better request completeness, routing transactions to appropriately skilled staff, reducing unnecessary handoffs, standardizing high-performing practices, and targeting the process steps responsible for the largest delay.

## Control
A sustainable control design would combine cycle-time/SLA KPIs with appropriate control charts, process ownership, standard work, exception monitoring and periodic segmentation to ensure improvements persist.

## Advanced-statistics extension
The broader course adds SPC, capability analysis, Taguchi loss, queueing and factorial DOE/ANOVA. This makes the project evidence of process analytics and statistical improvement—not merely process mapping.
